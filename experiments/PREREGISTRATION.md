## 2. Splits

- temporal: days 1 to 120 train, 121 to 180 test.
- clinic: 20 clinics train, 10 test (by profile: 10, 7 and 3 train).
- attack: as temporal, but attack types 6 and 10 are removed from training.
- user: two thirds of users train, the rest test, over all days.

Training uses all staff accesses of the training part, attacks included (they are unlabelled for
every method except the upper bound). Evaluation uses staff accesses of the test part, including
denied accesses. Break-glass accesses are excluded from the budgeted metrics for every method
because they always go to the 24-hour review queue. For the same reason, attack type 8 (break-glass
abuse) is excluded from H2, H3 and H4 and is reported separately as review-queue outcomes.

## 3. Methods

- B0 rules, B1 global IsolationForest, B2 per-role IsolationForest, B3 sequence VAE, B4 co-access
  collaborative filtering: raw-feature baselines.
- B5 explanation only (unexplained first, latest first): not a raw-feature baseline; tested
  against E2D in H5.
- E2D: gate (sigma < 0.5, a forgery flag, or break-glass), then IsolationForest per clinic with a
  global fallback, over behaviour, explanation, forgery and graph features. Gated-out accesses get
  score 0 and are never alerted.
- Upper bound: LightGBM trained on labelled training attacks. A ceiling only; never tested
  against.

Forgery flags are computed 24 hours after each access, as the nightly rescoring job would.
Template parameters are those in `packages/e2d-core/src/e2d_core/explain/templates.yaml`.

## 4. Budgets and metrics

Budgets B = 3, 5 and 10 alerts per clinic per day; B = 5 is primary.

For each method and each test clinic-day, the alerts are the B accesses with the highest score
above 0 (fewer if fewer have a score above 0). Ties are broken by access event id.

- recall@B: attack accesses that are alerts / all attack accesses, pooled over all test
  clinic-days of the split. The unit is the access event.
- precision@B: attack accesses that are alerts / alerts raised. The number of alerts raised is
  reported next to it.
- PR-AUC over all scored test accesses; campaign detection rate (a campaign counts as detected if
  any of its accesses is an alert); alerts per 1,000 accesses; explanation coverage of benign
  accesses; false-explanation rate of non-forging attacks (types 1 to 7 and 9).
- Hours to first alert: per campaign, from its first access to the time its first alert could be
  raised. An alert is timed when every feature it used is available. Every E2D and upper-bound
  score uses the forgery flags, so every E2D and upper-bound alert is timed 24 hours after the
  access. Alerts of B0 to B5 are timed at the access.

The code in `experiments/src/e2d_experiments/evaluate.py` implements these definitions.

## 5. Hypotheses and tests

Alpha is 0.05 for every test. Means are reported with 95% intervals from two bootstraps (10,000
resamples each): over seeds, and a cluster bootstrap over test clinics pooled across seeds.

**H1, coverage.** On the temporal split, the templates explain at least 90% of benign accesses
(mean coverage >= 0.90), and the false-explanation rate of non-forging attacks is below 10% (mean
< 0.10). Descriptive: both depend on the realism parameters in section 1, and both intervals are
reported.

**H2, detection.** Primary (confirmatory) test: on the temporal split at B = 5, E2D recall@B is
higher than that of the raw-feature baseline (B0 to B4) with the highest mean recall@B on that
split and budget. Test: one-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank over seeds, alpha 0.05, no
correction. Secondary tests: the same comparison for the other 11 split and budget pairs (4 splits x
3 budgets, minus the primary), Holm-corrected together. Effect size: matched-pairs rank-biserial r.
Zero differences are dropped (Wilcoxon's method) and the number dropped is reported.

**H3, hard attacks.** The recall@5 gain of E2D over the best raw-feature baseline (as chosen for
H2, temporal split, B = 5) is larger for types 1, 2 and 6 at high mimicry (m >= 1/2) than for the
other non-forging types at high mimicry (3, 4, 5, 7 and 9). Test: per seed, mean gain over
{1, 2, 6} minus mean gain over {3, 4, 5, 7, 9}, each mean taken over the types that have at least
one high-mimicry campaign in that seed's test part; one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank against 0 over
seeds. A seed where either group has no such campaign is dropped and reported.

**H4, forgery.** Forgery features restore detection of type 10: recall@5 for type 10 with E2D is
higher than with E2D without forgery features (ablation `no_forgery`). Test: one-sided paired
Wilcoxon over seeds, on the temporal and attack splits, Holm-corrected over the two.

**H5, value of the residual detector.** On the temporal split at B = 5, E2D recall@B is higher
than B5 (explanation only). Test: one-sided paired Wilcoxon over seeds, alpha 0.05. If H5 is not
supported, the paper states that the gain comes from the explanation layer, not the residual
detector.

## 6. Reporting

Every number in the paper comes from `make exp` outputs and is written to `paper/tables` and
`paper/figures` by `e2d_experiments.export`. All four splits, all three budgets and all five
hypotheses are reported, whether or not they are supported.

## 7. Ablations (descriptive)

`no_forgery`, `no_graph`, role conditioning (`none` = per clinic, `role_onehot`, `role_per_role`
with equal training size per role), learning curves (`learning_curve_10`, `_25`, `_50`, and 100%
as `role_per_role`), `templates_core`, mimicry (`mimicry_0`, `mimicry_05`, `mimicry_1`, which
simulate the data again at a fixed level), behaviour window (`window_1h`, `window_24h`,
`window_7d`), hard negatives (`hard_neg_half`, `hard_neg_double`, which simulate the data again
with the hard-negative scenario rates at half and double their v1 values; the measured share is
reported, because it comes out close to half or double but not exactly). Also E2D with
ECOD and COPOD scorers.

## 8. What would count against E2D

The primary H2 test not supported (temporal split, B = 5); or H5 not supported; or the upper bound
showing that the features carry little signal for some types (reported per type); or coverage
below 90%, which would mean the templates do not fit the simulated workflow; or E2D's advantage
disappearing in `hard_neg_double`.

## Known limits to state in the paper

- The false-explanation part of H1 mostly reflects `incidental_share`, so it checks the template
  engine and is not a finding.
- Hours to first alert favours B0 to B5 by design, because E2D and the upper bound alert 24 hours
  later.
- `no_progress` and `cancelled_after_access` are not defined for care-team evidence, so care-team
  forgeries (type 10) are the case the forgery flags do not target, and E2D must rely on its other
  features there.
- `self_created_recent` fires on 15% of benign staff accesses (mean 0.150 over the 10 seeds in
  experiments/audit/v1/summary.json), because the normal workflow often has the same user create
  the evidence shortly before opening the record (for example a receptionist booking and then
  opening the patient's details), so it barely separates forgeries (ROC-AUC 0.52).
- `off_path_creation` fires on 10% of type-10 accesses (mean 0.103; 0.00 to 0.24 per seed),
  because forgers pick patients who already had visits, often with the same doctor, and a
  receptionist's forged appointment is a reception booking.

## Pre-registration history

These changes were made before registration, after seeing a diagnostic from the separability
audit, the realism measures or the forgery flag table of `make prereg-audit`. No E2D or baseline
result had been computed when any of them was made: no detection method was fitted on SaathiBench
v1 before this commit.

- f2d7beb: a patient registration no longer sets `self_created_recent` (registering and then
  opening the record is the normal front-desk path). Made after seeing that registrations set the
  flag for ordinary front-desk accesses.
- 6a60426: simulator bug fix. Follow-ups booked into a later session of the same day were dropped,
  so the visit never happened and the appointment stayed booked. Found while checking the no-show
  and cancellation share in the realism table.
- 84cbae1: simulator fix. Forged appointments (type 10) stayed booked after their slot, which no
  real appointment does. They are now resolved like real ones (no-show, or cancelled by the forger
  with probability m). Found after the follow-up fix.
- 784bdc0: simulator fix. Status history (`status_events`) for appointments, lab orders, referrals
  and invoices; real lab orders include uncollected ones (`lab_uncollected_rate` 0.05), cancelled
  at closing time; forged lab orders are made by lab technicians (a lab order only explains a lab
  technician's access, so a doctor's forged order could never explain the access) and are
  cancelled by the forger (probability m) or at closing time; antenatal patients registered during
  the run plan their visits; a walk-in is checked in no earlier than its appointment is created.
  Found while checking how type-10 accesses were explained.
- 459ad3a: explanations use each record's status at the access time, and the forgery flags use its
  status 24 hours after the access, from the status history (before, both used the final status).
  In the same commit, `off_path_creation` was redefined as: the evidence was booked by a clinician
  (not reception) for a patient who has no completed encounter with that clinician before the
  booking. The redefinition was made after seeing the forgery flag AUC table, where the old
  clinic-level rule never fired. It was made once and kept whatever its AUC turned out to be.
- c207c0e: simulator change. With probability m, a doctor who forges an appointment now runs a fake
  visit at the slot (checks the patient in, opens the consultation, writes and signs a short note,
  completes the appointment) instead of cancelling it; otherwise it becomes a no-show as before.
  Receptionist and lab order forgeries are unchanged. Made after seeing the forgery flag table,
  where appointment forgeries were almost always flagged (cancelled_after_access 1.00,
  no_progress 0.97) because the simulated forger never covered their tracks. It lowers the flag
  AUCs by design: it makes the forgery test harder, never easier.
- 491fed4: an encounter counts against `off_path_creation` only if its appointment was not
  cancelled or a no-show at the access time (from the status history), never by its final status.
  The audit now fails a run in which any record lacks a full status history.

## 9. Deviations

Any change after registration is listed here with its reason and effect.

- Resumable runs (asked for by the authors before the final runs). The runner saves each finished
  seed's results under `<out>/seeds/` and reuses them when a stopped run is started again with the
  same settings. Reason: the final runs take many hours. Effect on results: none; a reused seed
  has exactly the rows it had when it was computed (test: `experiments/tests/test_resume.py`).
- B3 memory fix (approved by the authors after the first main run stopped with an out-of-memory
  error while fitting B3 on seed 0). B3 built the five-access sequence of every training access
  (4.06 million on seed 0, several full-size float64 copies) and then kept a sample of 100,000.
  It now draws the same 100,000 rows with the same generator call first and builds sequences only
  for them, and normalises the test sequences in place. Effect: none on the method; the
  sequences, the sampled rows, the normalisation and the scores are bit-for-bit those of the old
  code (`experiments/tests/test_sequence_vae.py` compares against the old implementation). On
  seed 0's temporal split B3 now fits in 16.5 s and scores 2.2 million accesses in 7.1 s on CPU,
  adding about 1.3 GB of memory. B3 stays on the CPU, as registered.
- Where and how the final runs were executed (no effect on the method). The final runs ran on a
  rented Linux machine (46 CPU cores, about 190 GB of RAM) from commit e74e66f, with the locked
  package versions (CPU PyTorch; no GPU code was used). Before the main run, seed 0 was simulated
  there and its separability audit matched `experiments/audit/v1/audit-seed0.json` exactly. Every
  final run used 4 threads per process (`POLARS_MAX_THREADS`, `OMP_NUM_THREADS`,
  `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS`, `MKL_NUM_THREADS`) and 4 worker processes per seed, with seeds run as
  separate processes and combined afterwards from the saved per-seed results. Thread counts can
  change floating-point results in the last digits, so all reported runs use this one setting.
- Ablations that use the main run's data unchanged (`no_forgery`, `no_graph`, `role_onehot`,
  `role_per_role`, `learning_curve_10/25/50`, `window_1h/24h/7d`) were run for E2D only, and the
  scorer variants (E2D with ECOD and COPOD) for those two methods only (approved by the authors
  before the final runs). These ablations change only E2D's features or training, so B0 to B5 and
  the upper bound would be refitted on identical data with identical settings; their results are
  those of the main run. `templates_core` changes the explanations, so E2D, B5 and the upper bound
  were run there, and only B0 to B4 come from the main run.
- `mimicry_0` was run without the separability-audit gate (approved by the authors during the
  final runs). Re-simulated with every campaign at mimicry 0, its data fails the registered audit
  on all 10 seeds (`off_shift` ROC-AUC 0.969 to 0.979 and depth-2 tree 0.972 to 0.982, threshold
  0.9; `experiments/results/v1/records/v1-mimicry_0-*/audit.json`), so the pipeline refused to
  use it. All 50 other simulated runs passed. Section 7 lists it as a descriptive ablation, so it was run with
  `data.require_audit=false` and its results are reported as outside the audited regime, together
  with its audit results. No other run was exempted from the gate.
- The measured hard-negative share of `hard_neg_half` and `hard_neg_double` (section 7) was not
  written by the pipeline, so it was measured after the runs with `saathibench.realism`, from the
  same simulated data (`experiments/results/v1/execution/extra.py`, output in
  `experiments/results/v1/diagnostics.json`): 0.016 (0.015 to 0.017) and 0.049 (0.048 to 0.050)
  over the 10 seeds, against 0.027 (0.026 to 0.028) in the main run. Three `hard_neg_half` runs
  simulated again on another machine had the same configuration fingerprint and row counts and
  gave the same shares. The same script reports two diagnostics that are not registered results:
  the false-explanation rate per attack type, and E2D's PR-AUC per clinic next to the pooled one.
  A second script (`experiments/results/v1/execution/extra2.py`, output in
  `experiments/results/v1/diagnostics2.json`) reports which template explains the falsely
  explained attacks, the distribution of explanation strength, coverage per role and what E2D's
  benign alerts at B = 5 are; it also recomputed E2D's recall@5 on every seed and matched the
  registered value exactly. Figures for the paper are drawn from the committed results by
  `e2d_experiments.paper_figures`, which was added after the runs and computes no new result
  (the per-seed paired differences it plots are the ones the registered tests use).
