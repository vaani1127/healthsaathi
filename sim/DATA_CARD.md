# SaathiBench data card (draft)

Status: draft for v1.0. The attack part (P13) is not in the data yet. The release licence and the
Zenodo and Hugging Face records are still to be decided by the authors.

## Summary

SaathiBench is a synthetic dataset of outpatient (OPD) clinic workflow and of staff accesses to
patient records, for small Indian clinics. It is written in the same table shapes as the
HealthSaathi product database (`docs/SPEC.md` section 3), so the same code can explain and score
accesses in the product and in the benchmark. Every row is generated by a program. There is no real
patient, no real staff member and no real clinic in it.

## How it is made

- Generator: `sim/src/saathibench` (SimPy discrete-event simulation), run with
  `make sim CONFIG=sim/configs/v1.yaml`.
- Run config `sim/configs/v1.yaml`: 30 clinics (15 solo GP, 10 polyclinic, 5 nursing home), 180
  days from 2026-01-05, seed 20261008, timezone Asia/Kolkata.
- Clinic profiles `sim/profiles/*.yaml`: staff counts, morning and evening sessions, Saturday and
  Sunday sessions, slot length, patients per doctor session, walk-in share, no-show and
  cancellation rates, share of chronic and antenatal patients, lab order and referral rates,
  health camp interval, cover days and hard negative rates. All of these are design choices for the
  simulation, not measurements of real clinics.
- Each clinic has its own random generator seeded from the run seed and the clinic's position, so
  the output is the same for the same config whatever the number of worker processes.
- Allow and deny decisions and `policy_version` come from the product's own policy file
  (`apps/api/app/access/policy.yaml`).

### Workflow

1. A population is registered before the first day; chronic and antenatal patients already have a
   doctor, a care-team assignment and a due date.
2. Each session, appointments are booked ahead by reception or by patients through the portal;
   doctors book follow-ups at the end of a consultation. Walk-ins arrive during the session.
3. A visit goes through reception check-in (queue token), the nurse (vitals), the doctor
   (encounter, note, prescription, sometimes a lab order or a referral) and billing (invoice and
   usually a payment). Queues use SimPy resources, so waiting times depend on load.
4. Lab technicians collect samples, enter results and release them; the ordering doctor reviews
   the result in a later session. Referred doctors open the chart within a few days.
5. Patients with portal accounts sometimes look at their own record after a visit.

Every look at or change to a record writes an `access_events` row with user, role, patient,
resource, action, time, session, device, network hash and decision.

### Benign hard negatives

Normal behaviour that is expected to look unusual. Accesses that belong to one of these are listed
in `labels/benign_scenarios` (`access_event_id`, `scenario`).

| Scenario | What happens |
|---|---|
| `cover_doctor` | A doctor is away; a colleague sees their patients. Reception moves only some appointments to the covering doctor. |
| `after_hours_emergency` | At night a doctor opens a patient's emergency view through break-glass, from a phone at home. |
| `nurse_lab_followup` | The morning after results, a nurse opens the patient's details and vitals; sometimes they also try the lab result, which the policy denies. |
| `pharmacy_check` | A nurse checks allergies of a patient who is collecting medicines without a visit that day. |
| `month_end_billing` | On the last working day of the month reception goes through many of the month's invoices, and the clinic admin exports some. |
| `staff_self_view` | A staff member who is also a patient of the clinic opens their own record with their staff role. |

Health camps (many new registrations in one morning) are part of the normal workflow and are not
labelled.

## Contents

```
<run>/tables/<table>/<clinic>-<part>.parquet   product table shapes (sim/src/saathibench/schema.py)
<run>/labels/benign_scenarios/...              labels, kept apart from the tables
<run>/manifest.json                            config, seed, policy version, row counts, timings
```

Tables: clinics, users, memberships, staff_profiles, patients, devices, sessions, services,
schedules, shifts, appointments, queue_tokens, encounters, referrals, care_team_assignments,
vitals, allergies, conditions, clinical_notes, prescriptions, lab_orders, lab_results, invoices,
payments, consents, break_glass_events, access_events. Ids are UUIDv7 strings and times are UTC.
Row counts for a run are in its `manifest.json`.

Feature and detector code must never read `labels/`. The attack labels added in P13 go there too.

## Checks

`sim/tests/test_simulator.py` checks, on a short run: the table shapes, that every value is a valid
product value, that decisions follow the product policy, that every reference points at an existing
row, that staff only act in roles they hold, that identities are synthetic, that the hard negatives
appear, and that the same seed gives the same output.

Explanation coverage per role and per scenario is printed by
`make sim-coverage RUN=sim/output/<name>`. Coverage figures are only reported from that script's
output.

## Known limits

- The tables are a snapshot at the end of the run. A row that changed during the run (an
  appointment that was later completed, an invoice paid at month end) shows only its final state,
  with `updated_at` set to the last change. Referrals stay `active` until they expire.
- There is no visit history before the first day, so templates that need past visits (follow-ups)
  are rarer in the first weeks.
- Clinical values (vitals, drugs, tests) are plausible ranges and lists, not a clinical model.
- Names come from short lists of common given names and surnames (`data/names.yaml`) combined at
  random; phone numbers, addresses and emails are random and use the reserved `.test` domain. No
  ABHA numbers are generated.
- Staff behaviour is simpler than in real clinics: one desk device per staff member, a phone for
  doctors, one session per user per day and device.

## Intended use

Research on explaining and auditing access to health records, and testing of the HealthSaathi
product. It is not suitable for clinical or epidemiological conclusions.
